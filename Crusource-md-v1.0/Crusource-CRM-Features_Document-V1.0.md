# Crusource CRM — Features

## What Is This Platform?

The CRM is a web-based system that helps a sales team manage the entire sales process in one place — from the moment a potential customer is identified, all the way through to closing a deal and keeping a record of every conversation along the way.

The platform covers the core sales cycle, plus a set of AI-assisted capabilities layered on top:

- **Leads** — potential customers who haven't been qualified yet
- **Contacts** — the individual people you deal with
- **Accounts** — the companies or organizations those people belong to
- **Deals** — active sales opportunities you're trying to close
- **Documents** — files attached to accounts or deals
- **Activities** — the tasks, meetings, and calls that make up day-to-day sales work
- **Reports & Analytics** — a view into how the sales team and pipeline are performing
- **AI Features** — scoring, drafting, risk alerts, and summarization that reduce manual work
- **User & Role Management** — controlling who can see and do what

Below, each area is explained in terms of what a user can actually do — not the technical build.

## Getting Into the System

- Everyone in the company works inside a single shared organization — there's no mixing with other companies' data.
- Nobody can sign themselves up. An Admin has to add each new employee to the system first.
- When an Admin adds someone, they assign that person one of three roles:
  - **Admin** — sees and manages everything
  - **Sales Manager** — sees their own work plus their team's work
  - **Sales Rep** — sees only their own work
- Logging in is a simple email + password screen.

This role structure isn't just about login access — it also controls what each person is allowed to see once they're inside (more on this under Roles & Access).

## Pricing — Trial & Subscription

Every new organization starts with a 14-day free trial, with full access to the platform. Once the trial ends, the organization needs an active paid subscription to keep using the CRM.

## Getting Around the System

The system is organized around a sidebar on the left, so every part of the CRM is always one click away:

- **Home** — a personal dashboard, described below
- **Workqueues** — a working list view for prioritizing what needs attention
- **Reports** — the reporting area
- **Sales section** — Leads, Contacts, Accounts, Deals, Documents
- **Activities section** — Tasks, Meetings, Calls
- **Settings** — only visible to Admins, for managing users and roles

At the top of every screen, users also have:

- A search bar that can look across Leads, Contacts, Accounts, and Deals at once, so a user doesn't need to know which module a record lives in.
- A quick-create (+) button that lets a user add a new Lead, Contact, Account, Deal, Document, Task, Meeting, or Call from anywhere in the system, without navigating away from what they're doing.
- Notifications, alerting users when a task is due or something is assigned to them.
- A calendar icon, showing upcoming meetings and task deadlines at a glance.
- A profile menu for account settings and logging out.

## Home Dashboard — "Your Day at a Glance"

The moment a user logs in, they land on a personal Home dashboard that summarizes what matters to them right now. Importantly, this dashboard automatically adjusts to the person's role:

- A Sales Rep sees only their own numbers.
- A Sales Manager sees their whole team's numbers, and can open any individual rep's personal dashboard directly.
- An Admin sees numbers for the entire organization.

The dashboard shows:

- **Key numbers at a glance:** how many deals are open, how many deals haven't had any activity logged in a while (a warning sign), how many leads belong to them, and how many calls they have today.
- **My Open Tasks** — a quick list of what's due, its priority, and who it relates to.
- **My Meetings** — upcoming meetings with times and who they're with.
- **Today's Leads** — new leads that have come in today, with contact details and where they came from.
- **Deals Closing This Month** — a forward-looking list of deals expected to close soon, with amounts and stages, so nothing slips through the cracks.

## Leads — Capturing and Qualifying Potential Customers

A Lead is someone who has shown interest but hasn't been vetted as a real sales opportunity yet. The Leads section is where that vetting happens.

**Why it matters:** Uncaptured leads are lost revenue. This module makes sure every lead is logged, owned, and moved toward a decision.

**What a user can do here:**

- View all leads the organization has collected, or switch to a filtered view: just their own leads, only open (unqualified) leads, leads that have already been converted, or leads created/updated recently.
- Add a new lead manually, capturing the person's name, company, email, phone number, and where the lead came from (e.g. cold email, referral, website).
- Import a batch of leads at once from a spreadsheet, rather than entering them one by one — useful after a trade show or marketing campaign.
- Track a lead's status as it moves through New → Qualified → Converted, or mark it as Junk if it's not a real opportunity.
- Filter and sort the list to find specific leads quickly (e.g. all leads from "Referral" that are still "New").
- Convert a qualified lead into a real sales opportunity. This is the key action in this module — with one click, the system automatically creates (or links to) the associated Account (company) and Contact (person), and creates a new Deal to start tracking the opportunity.
- Switch between a simple list view and a visual pipeline board, where leads appear as cards grouped by status, so a user can see at a glance how many leads are sitting in each stage.

## Contacts — Managing the People You Work With

Contacts are the individual people a sales rep is building a relationship with — the humans behind the deals.

**Why it matters:** Linking every contact to their company keeps relationships intact even when a rep leaves.

**What a user can do here:**

- View all contacts, or narrow down to just their own contacts, or recently created/updated ones.
- Add a new contact, recording their name, the company they work for (linked to that company's Account record), email, and phone number.
- Import a list of contacts in bulk.
- Filter and sort contacts to find someone quickly.
- Review previous conversations with a contact and get an AI-generated summary of what's been discussed, without having to scroll back through every past interaction.
- Because each contact is linked to an Account, a user can always see which company someone belongs to and jump between the two.

## Accounts — Managing the Companies You Sell To

An Account represents a company or organization — the business entity a deal is ultimately made with.

**Why it matters:** One page per company gives anyone full context on that relationship instantly.

**What a user can do here:**

- View all accounts, or filter to their own or recently touched ones.
- Add a new account, recording the company name, phone number, website, and who on the sales team owns that relationship.
- AI-powered duplicate detection: if a sales rep tries to add a company that already exists in the system, the AI flags it and asks whether they're referring to the existing account, instead of creating a duplicate record.
- Import accounts in bulk.
- Open an account's detail page to see everything connected to that company in one place, organized into tabs:
  - **Contacts** — every person at that company
  - **Deals** — every opportunity tied to that company, past and present
  - **Activities** — every task, meeting, and call ever logged against that company
- Filter and sort the accounts list.

This gives a complete 360-degree view of a customer relationship — instead of hunting through separate lists, everything about a company lives on one page.

## Deals — Managing the Sales Pipeline

A Deal is an active sales opportunity — this is where revenue is won or lost, and it's the heart of the CRM.

**Why it matters:** A standardized set of stages keeps pipeline and revenue forecasting consistent across every rep.

**What a user can do here:**

- View all deals, with the deal name, dollar amount, current stage, expected closing date, the company and contact involved, and who owns it.
- Add a new deal manually, or have one created automatically when a Lead is converted.
- Move a deal through a defined sales pipeline with six stages, each carrying a built-in win-probability so forecasting stays consistent across the team
  - Qualification
  - Discovery (Needs Analysis)
  - Proposal
  - Negotiation
  - Closed Won
  - Closed Lost
- Customizable pipeline: the stages above are the default setup, but an Admin can adjust the stage names, order, or win probabilities to fit how the business actually sells.
- Use a visual pipeline board, where deals appear as draggable cards grouped by stage. Each column shows the total dollar value sitting in that stage, and a user can simply drag a deal from one stage to the next as it progresses — no forms required.
- Switch to a traditional list view for a straightforward, spreadsheet-style look at every deal.
- Import deals in bulk, and filter/sort to focus on specific segments (e.g. everything closing this month, or everything stuck in Negotiation).
- Track the next follow-up and last interaction for every deal, so a rep or manager can see at a glance when a deal was last touched and when it's due for its next touchpoint.

## Documents — Keeping Files Organized Against the Business

The Documents section is a simple file library tied directly to the sales records they belong to.

**Why it matters:** Attaching files to the account or deal keeps paperwork accessible to the whole team, not just the person who uploaded it.

**What a user can do here:**

- Upload a document and link it to the relevant Account or Deal (e.g. a signed proposal, a contract, a pricing sheet).
- View all documents, seeing the file name, what it's related to, and when it was uploaded.
- Filter and sort to find a specific file quickly.
- Delete documents that are no longer needed.

This keeps important paperwork attached to the business context it belongs to, instead of scattered across email or shared drives.

Looking ahead: version control for documents is on the roadmap, so future updates to a file can be tracked and earlier versions revisited.

## Activities — Tasks, Meetings, and Calls

"Activities" is the umbrella term for the day-to-day work of selling: the things a rep actually does. It's split into three types, and — importantly — every single one can be linked back to whichever Lead, Contact, Account, or Deal it relates to. This means a full history of "everything that happened" is always visible on the record it relates to (see Activity Timeline below).

**Why it matters:** Logging every task, meeting, and call keeps engagement history intact and visible to managers.

### 1. Tasks

To-do items with a subject, a due date, a status (Not Started / In Progress / Completed), and a priority level (Low / Normal / High / Highest).

- Create a task, assign it, and link it to a contact and to the relevant Lead/Account/Deal.
- Import tasks in bulk.
- Mark a task as complete once it's done.
- Filter to see what's outstanding.

### 2. Meetings

Scheduled meetings with a title, start and end time, who's attending, and who's hosting.

- Schedule a meeting, linking it to the relevant contact and record.
- Filter the meetings list to find upcoming or past meetings.
- Allow the sales rep to import the transcription or meeting minitus

### 3. Calls

A log of phone calls, whether inbound or outbound, with the call's start time, how long it lasted, and who it was with.

- Log a call after it happens (or schedule one), recording the type, duration, and contact.
- Filter the calls list.

### 4. Notes

A simple, free-form space for jotting down details that don't belong to a specific task, meeting, or call.

- Add a note to any Lead, Contact, Account, or Deal, so quick context isn't lost or left sitting in someone's inbox.
- View all notes logged against a record, right alongside its other activity on the timeline.

### Activity Timeline

Every Lead, Contact, Account, and Deal has its own page — and on that page, a chronological timeline shows every Task, Meeting, and Call ever logged against it. This means anyone picking up a relationship — a manager stepping in, or a rep covering for a colleague — can see the complete history of engagement in one scroll, instead of piecing it together from memory or inbox searches.

## Reports — Pre-Built Answers to Common Questions

**Why it matters:** Gives the team instant answers to common sales questions, without manual reporting.

The Reports area gives the team ready-made answers to common sales questions, organized into folders:

| Folder | What It Answers |
| --- | --- |
| Lead Reports | Where are our leads coming from, and what status are they in? |
| Deal Reports | How is the pipeline distributed across stages, and what's our win/loss rate? |
| Activity Reports | What activities are still outstanding, and which tasks are overdue? |
| Sales Rep Performance Reports | How is each Sales Rep performing individually, for a Sales Manager reviewing their team? (Available to Sales Managers and Admins.) |

In Version 1, these six reports come built-in and ready to use. There isn't yet a way for a user to build their own custom report from scratch — that's a capability planned for a future version.

## Analytics Dashboard — The Big Picture

**Why it matters:** Gives leadership a real-time pulse on sales health, so problems surface early enough to act on.

Separate from personal reports, there's a single organization-level Home analytics dashboard that gives leadership a snapshot of overall health:

- **Open Deals** — how many deals are open right now, and their combined value
- **Pipeline by Stage** — how deals are distributed across the sales stages, by count and value
- **Leads This Month** — how many new leads came in, and whether that's trending up or down versus last month
- **Activity Stats** — a summary of open tasks, overdue tasks, completed tasks, calls made, and meetings held

## AI Features — Working Smarter, Not Harder

This is a set of AI-assisted capabilities layered on top of the core CRM. They're designed around one principle: the AI never acts on its own. It scores, drafts, flags, and summarizes — but a person always reviews and decides before anything is sent, dismissed, or acted on. Nothing here creates a new access rule either; if a user can already see a record, they can already see its AI output.

### Smart Lead Scoring

**Why it matters:** Automatic scoring tells reps which leads to prioritize, instead of guessing.

- Every open lead is automatically scored from 0–100, based on signals like where it came from, how quickly it's being followed up on, and its current status.
- Alongside the score, the system gives a short plain-language reason (e.g. "Referral source, contacted within 1 hour"), so a rep understands why a lead ranks the way it does — not just a mystery number.
- The score appears right on the Leads list and pipeline board, and can be sorted on, so the highest-priority leads always rise to the top.
- Scores refresh automatically overnight, and instantly whenever a lead's status changes or a new activity is logged against it — so the number is never stale.
- Customizable scoring: an Admin can adjust how points are assigned across signals, so the scoring model can be tuned to match the organization's own sense of what makes a lead valuable.

### AI-Drafted Follow-up Emails

**Why it matters:** Gives reps a fast starting draft for routine follow-ups, with a human still reviewing before it's sent.

- From any Lead, Contact, or Deal, a rep can click "Draft with AI" to generate a follow-up email written using that record's details and its recent activity history.
- The draft appears in an editable box — the rep can tweak it, regenerate it if it misses the mark, or discard it entirely.
- Nothing is ever sent automatically. The rep always reviews and sends it themselves through the normal process.

### Deal Risk Alerts

**Why it matters:** Surfaces at-risk deals before they're lost to neglect.

- The system automatically flags a deal when it's gone quiet (no activity logged in about a week) or when its closing date is approaching without recent progress.
- Each flag comes with a short, specific reason (e.g. "No activity in 9 days, Closing Date in 5 days"), so a rep knows exactly what to act on.
- Flags show up on the Deals list, the pipeline board, and directly in the "Untouched Deals" widget on the Home dashboard — so managers can see which deals across the team need attention, not just a total count.
- A rep can dismiss a flag if they judge it a false alarm, or jump straight into logging an activity from the flagged deal to clear it properly.

### Meeting & Call Summarization

**Why it matters:** Turns raw notes into a quick, scannable summary.

- After logging a meeting or call, a rep can type or paste in notes (or a transcript).
- The system generates a short summary plus a suggested next step, shown right on the record.
- On the shared Activity Timeline, the AI summary is what's shown by default — keeping the timeline scannable — while the full original notes are always one click away.
- The suggested next step can be turned into a follow-up task with a single click, so nothing gets lost between "we should follow up on this" and it actually happening.

### Conversational AI Assistant (RAG Chatbot)

**Why it matters:** Lets reps and managers get answers instantly, without digging through screens.

- A chat panel, available anywhere in the CRM, lets reps and managers ask questions in plain language.
- The assistant answers using the org's own CRM data — deals, activities, contacts, documents — not generic outside knowledge.
- Answers stay scoped to what that person can already see (same Own/Team/Full rules as the rest of the CRM).
- Every answer links back to its source record, so nothing has to be taken on faith.

**Planned future integrations:**

- Daily task reminders, sent proactively through the chat instead of a rep having to go looking.
- Summaries of requested data, so a rep can ask for a quick rundown instead of pulling it together manually.
- End-of-day wrap-up: when a rep logs off, they tell the assistant their completed tasks and tomorrow's plan right in the chat, and the AI automatically builds tomorrow's Workqueue from it.

## User & Role Management (Admin Only)

This area, found under Settings, is where an Admin controls who has access to the system and what they're allowed to do.

**Why it matters:** Lets Admins manage access safely as the team grows or changes, without ever losing any data.

**What an Admin can do here:**

- See a full list of every user in the organization, their email, their assigned role, and whether their account is active or inactive.
- Add a new user to the organization and assign them a role right away.
- Change someone's role if their responsibilities change (e.g. promoting a Sales Rep to Sales Manager).
- Deactivate a user to immediately cut off their login access — without deleting any of the records or history they created. This is important for when someone leaves the company: their work stays intact, but they can no longer get in.
- Assign each Sales Rep to a Sales Manager (a "Reports To" relationship), which is what determines what a Sales Manager can see as their "team."
- Transfer data between accounts: move a user's records — leads, contacts, accounts, deals, and activities — from one user to another, so ownership stays clean when someone changes roles, changes territory, or leaves the company.

## Roles & Access — Who Can See What

Access in the CRM follows a simple, consistent rule across every module:

In plain terms:

- A Sales Rep only ever sees the records they personally own.
- A Sales Manager sees their own records plus the records of every Sales Rep who reports to them. A Sales Manager can also open any of their reps' individual dashboards, step in and perform Sales Rep actions on their behalf when needed, assign tasks directly to reps, and generate reports on each rep's performance — giving them full team visibility and hands-on control without needing to ask each rep individually for status updates.
- An Admin sees and can manage everything in the organization — including every Sales Rep's data and accounts — with no restrictions, and is the only role that can manage other users.

This means the same CRM screen shows different amounts of data depending on who's logged in — a rep gets a focused personal view, while a manager or admin gets progressively broader visibility, without needing separate systems or manual reporting.

## Feedback & Feature Requests

Any user can submit feedback about the CRM or request a specific feature, right from within the system, so the product team hears directly from the people using it every day and can prioritize what matters most.

## Summary — What Does the System Actually Deliver?

At a glance, the CRM lets a sales organization:

1. Capture interest through Leads.
2. Qualify and convert that interest into real opportunities.
3. Track those opportunities as Deals through a clear, consistent pipeline.
4. Maintain a full picture of every Contact and Account involved.
5. Log every task, meeting, and call so nothing about a relationship is ever lost.
6. Store related documents alongside the business context they belong to.
7. Work smarter with AI-driven scoring, drafting, risk alerts, summarization and a chatbot — always with a human making the final call.
8. Report and analyze performance, from an individual rep's day up to the whole organization's pipeline health.
9. Control access cleanly, so every person sees exactly what's relevant to their role — no more, no less.
